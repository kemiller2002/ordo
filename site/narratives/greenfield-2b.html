Interpretation

Two results here, and the second one is the reason this experiment matters more than its replication status suggests.

The convergence

Two agents, the same frozen requirements, no contact between them until one had frozen its own results entirely. They converged strongly on semantics: the same seven entities, the same three state machines with the same transitions, a mathematically identical overlap formula, an identical billing formula, the same four-outcome storage result, and the same architecture for deriving capabilities from the predicates the transitions enforce. Both documented the reason for that last one in nearly the same words.

They converged much less on physical structure — different file counts under most directories, two different shapes of dependency injection, one splitting its application layer five ways where the other split it two. The comparison is explicit that identical source-file counts are the least meaningful thing in it, and that the two builds' exactly-equal test line counts are almost certainly coincidence and are not used as evidence for anything.

Declining to use a striking number is a harder discipline than finding one.

The verification result

A fresh agent, with no knowledge of the construction, reviewed a build that had passed a 90-test suite, a strict type check, a dependency audit, an architecture check, and an adversarial self-review including three mutation tests that deliberately broke shared guards to confirm they were load-bearing.

Five findings. Zero overlap with any of it.

One was a result case declared in a type that no code path could construct. One was a tie-break favouring the wrong side of an exact timestamp equality. One was a pair of diagnostic categories that existed in the type system and were never actually surfaced. A fourth was dead code. The fifth was not a defect at all but a genuine interpretation ambiguity, which was recorded and deliberately left unresolved rather than settled by changing the product to match a guess.

The comparison's own treatment of the defect counts is the part worth carrying away. This build recorded seven defects against the other's two, and the comparison refuses to read that as a quality difference, because four of the seven exist only because a verification pass was run that the other build's protocol never included. Cut this build's process off where the other's ended and it recorded three.

Then it checked whether one of its own defect classes existed in the other build. It did, unaddressed — not because that build was careless, but because nothing in its protocol was shaped to find it.
