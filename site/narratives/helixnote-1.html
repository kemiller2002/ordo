Interpretation

The headline here is often read as "the compiler found two thirds of it". The more useful reading is the other third.

Four sites required a person to go looking, and the record of how they were found is the interesting part: database constraint archaeology, noticing a symmetry with a sibling function that had already been fixed, and reviewing test coverage. None of those is a mechanism. All three are a competent person being thorough, and none of them would have survived a tired afternoon.

The distribution explains itself. The one fact under the compiler's protection had zero duplicated copies. The two facts outside it had four and two. That is not a coincidence about this codebase — it is what happens to a fact that nothing forces to agree with itself.

The silent-omission probe is what turned this from a measurement into a research programme. Everything passed. The build, the tests, the runtime, the architecture check. A clinical fact was accepted into the system and written nowhere retrievable, and the only thing that noticed was an experiment deliberately looking for it.

This experiment set both of the directions everything after it went: mechanise what the type system can reach (Experiment 2), and find out what still gets through when you do (Experiment 2's Phase 20, and every trial since).
