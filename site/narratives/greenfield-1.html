Interpretation

This trial's contribution is not its output. It is that the instruments were built and frozen before the work started.

A metrics dictionary defining every metric's unit, collection mechanism, raw evidence source, derivation formula and known limitations was written first, with a rule that no definition changes without a changelog entry. Builds and tests then ran through wrappers that write immutable evidence pairs. The result is that the numbers here are readings rather than recollections — which is the difference between this trial and the first two, whose work metrics are explicitly labelled manual reconstructions.

The dictionary was also wrong about something, and that is worth more than most of what it got right. Its first version stated that per-session cost data was not available to a running agent. It is; the session API exposes it. The correction sits in the changelog next to the original claim rather than replacing it, because a dictionary that silently redefines its own metrics makes every before-and-after comparison meaningless.

The agent-error log is the other thing worth copying. Five reasoning errors recorded separately from defects — a misread requirement or an invented rule, whether or not it produced misbehaviour. One of them is a small masterpiece of the genre: a commit hook designed to append each commit's evidence to a tracked file, which requires another commit to record, forever. The agent's own self-review did not catch it. The user's tooling did.

The cost figure needs its caveat read. It covers two different tasks in one number, taken mid-session. It is published because it is real and rare; it is not a cost-per-feature figure and nothing on this site treats it as one.
