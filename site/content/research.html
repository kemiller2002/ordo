Research

Research status

Ordo is a methodology under active research, not a validated discipline. This page is the whole map: what is supported, what is provisional, what has been refuted, and what is simply unknown.

Two labels, never merged

A rule can be required by the methodology while the benefit it predicts remains unproven. Those are different statements and Ordo keeps them apart with two vocabularies.

Confidence class

How strongly the rule is held

REQUIRED — strong enough and fundamental enough that the methodology depends on it.
RECOMMENDED — evidence-backed or strongly justified, with contextual exceptions.
EXPERIMENTAL — promising, needs validation.
RESEARCH ONLY — not yet suitable as normal guidance.

Evidence state

What the evidence currently says

Supported — evidence establishes it within its stated scope.
Provisional — some evidence, or an adopted contract whose predicted benefit is untested.
Contradicted — measurement refuted it. Kept permanently.
Open — asked, not answered.

An adopted decision can make a contract REQUIRED. It cannot make the outcome that contract predicts Supported. Several rows below are exactly that pairing, and they are the rows most likely to be misread.

Why the refuted rows stay

{{count:contradicted}} propositions here are classified Contradicted. They are not deleted, and they are not moved to a footnote. They stay in the map permanently, cross-linked to the record that refuted them, so that a future engineer or agent cannot silently re-propose them without first seeing why they were rejected.

Two of them are the sort of thing a methodology would normally quietly stop mentioning. Boundary hardening was expected to reduce how much boundary work a change requires; measured three times across two architectures, it did not. A higher mechanical discovery rate was expected to mean lower cost; in one paired trial the two moved in opposite directions.

The least evidenced thing here

Ordo's unifying idea — that bounding how much must be inferred at once to make a safe change is what produces the benefit — is classified RESEARCH ONLY with an evidence state of Open (CL-016).

There is no causal comparison behind it. Task, model, tooling, familiarity and verification differences may dominate any effect entirely. It is the organising principle of the whole methodology and it is the weakest claim on this site, and putting those two sentences next to each other is the point of this page.

Open questions

  • Does explicit state reduce agent cost? Unknown. The one trial that measured it was interrupted, and its treatment-condition telemetry covers a partial run (CL-010).
  • Can the HTTP and API tier be mechanically guarded? A candidate mechanism is sketched in one source report. It has not been built or tested (CL-009).
  • Does the construction ordering work as a sequence? Its individual steps are supported; the ordering has never been trial-tested as one workflow on a real project (CL-013).
  • Do navigation artifacts pay for their maintenance? No outcome trial exists, and staleness may offset the benefit entirely (CL-015).
  • Is agreement between two agents evidence, or a shared blind spot? Every agent in every experiment here came from one model family. Two systems that fail the same way are not two mechanisms, and this evidence base cannot currently tell those cases apart.
  • Does any of this transfer? Four languages, one organisation, no team-scale observation, no counterfactual arm.

The standing rule

The repository that develops this methodology carries its own warning, and it applies to this site too: do not infer that State-Directed Engineering is a validated discipline, method or product merely because the process around it is rigorous. A careful process is evidence about the process.
