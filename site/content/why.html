Motivation

Why Ordo exists

Every rule in Ordo is a response to something that actually went wrong, in a codebase, in a way that was reproduced deliberately afterwards. This page lists those failures and points at the experiment that found each one.

A change compiles, passes every check, and does nothing

This is the failure that shaped the methodology more than any other. A boundary arm exists. It type-checks. The architecture check passes. The contract tests pass. The arm does no work, and nothing objects.

The first experiment removed a required fan-out arm on purpose and ran the build, the test suite and a direct database query. {{metric:M-HN1-SILENT}} produced any signal. The record was durably marked accepted while the fact it represented was never written anywhere retrievable.

The second experiment built five mechanisms specifically to close the first experiment's blind spots, then re-created the same failure shape against them. {{metric:M-HN2-NOOP}} caught it. Exhaustiveness checking guarantees the arm exists; it says nothing about whether the arm does anything. Only a live integration test found it.

The third experiment ran the same class of mission on both architectures with two independent agents. The identical semantic no-op appeared in both conditions and was caught, in both, by integration tests only.

Illegal combinations are representable, so someone has to remember

When a record carries a status string and three optional fields, nothing stops {status: "Recorded", supersededBy: [...]} from being constructed. It is meaningless, and it is legal to write.

Two independent builds of the same requirements handled this differently, and the difference is instructive. One modelled the entry as a discriminated union keyed by status, so a superseded-only field cannot be supplied on a recorded entry — enforced by the compiler at every construction site. The other used one flat record with optional fields and prevented the combination by discipline plus a load-time validator, and said so in its own self-review, unprompted, as the most honest limitation it had found in itself.

Both are defensible. The point is that one of them requires nobody to remember anything.

Boundaries lose meaning quietly

A closed set of named alternatives becomes a string when it crosses into the database, or onto the wire, or into another service. Nothing fails. The set is now open, and the agreement between what the domain permits and what the boundary admits is maintained by hand, in several places, with no mechanism that notices when they disagree.

Experiment 1 found exactly this: the one fact the compiler protects had zero duplication; the two facts outside its reach were duplicated four times and twice, with no automated agreement check between the copies. A separate investigation reproduced something sharper still — a single shared type, inside one process, producing three different wire behaviours depending on which call path reached it.

Whatever a shared type does for you, it does not do this.

Discovering what to change costs more than changing it

An agent or an engineer who does not know where a semantic decision lives reads broadly to find out. In the paired trial, the baseline condition performed {{metric:M-HN3-SEARCH}} — a 63% reduction under the hardened architecture, in one paired trial with two agents whose investigation strategies differed.

This is worth stating carefully, because the same trial refuted the obvious interpretation of it. See the next section.

Things this research expected and did not find

A motivation page that lists only confirmed problems is a sales page. Three expectations this programme held, and measured, and gave up:

  • That hardening would reduce how much boundary work a change requires. It did not. {{metric:M-HN2-BCA}} — measured three times, across three domain cases and two architectures, identically. Hardening changed how the work was found, not how much of it there was.
  • That a higher mechanical discovery rate means lower cost. In the paired trial the hardened condition scored {{metric:M-HN3-MDR}} on as-experienced discovery while simultaneously spending less on search and repair. The two proxies moved in opposite directions, in the same trial, on the same mission.
  • That explicit state reduces agent token cost. Unknown. {{metric:M-HN3-COST}} — the one trial that measured it was interrupted mid-run and its treatment-condition telemetry covers a partial run only. The question is open.

All three stay in the research map permanently, so that nobody can propose them again without first reading why they were rejected.

And one thing it did not expect to find

In the replication trial, a fresh agent with no knowledge of the construction reviewed a finished, self-reviewed, mutation-tested build. Of its five findings, {{metric:M-GF2B-OVERLAP}} were already covered by the builder's own 90-test suite or its own adversarial self-review.

One of them was a declared outcome that could never be constructed: a result case that existed in the type and that no code path could produce. The type checker was satisfied. The tests were satisfied. The contract was incomplete.

That is the strongest single argument this site can make for heterogeneous verification, and it is still one pass on one codebase.
