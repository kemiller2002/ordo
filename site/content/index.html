Ordo · State-Directed Engineering

Make the legal next move explicit.

Ordo is Echelon Foundry's engineering methodology for building software around explicit state, evidence, legal transitions, capabilities, obligations, and uncertainty. Instead of asking a human or an AI agent to infer what the system means, the domain states what is true, what may happen next, what is still unresolved, and what must be verified before work continues.

In one sentence

Bound the reasoning before you ask for a decision.

Give the engineer or agent the smallest semantically complete state view, the evidence and coverage that support it, the authority already established by the host, and the legal choices the domain permits. Then verify the result through mechanisms with different failure sensitivities.

Ordo is not an AI wrapper, not a generic workflow engine, and not a fifth architecture tier. It is a method for making software semantics explicit and mechanically enforceable.
The core idea

Give the system a vocabulary for truth, action, and uncertainty.

Most codebases encode these ideas indirectly through nullable fields, duplicated checks, comments, conventions, exceptions, and tribal knowledge. Ordo makes them first-class.

State

Represent what can actually be true

A domain uses closed alternatives and state-specific data rather than asking every caller to reconstruct meaning from combinations of fields. A bounded decision operates on the smallest domain-defined state view that is semantically complete for that decision.

The view is versioned and fingerprinted as a whole. Ordo does not selectively ignore changed fields.

Evidence and coverage

Represent what is known and how completely it is known

Evidence carries provenance. Coverage says whether a named scope is Complete, Partial, or Unknown. Required coverage can stop a decision before a provider or model is called.

More context, provider confidence, or a successful call never silently becomes proof of complete coverage.

Capabilities

Expose semantic authority, not a second rulebook

A Capability is a semantic authority prerequisite that the host has already established and supplied for transition evaluation. It is not authentication and it cannot be created by model output or confidence.

The same predicates that guard a transition should also determine whether that transition is available.

Obligations

Make unresolved work part of the state

If something must still happen before the system can proceed safely, Ordo represents that as an obligation instead of leaving it in a comment, a log message, or someone's memory.

Obligations can block later transitions until the domain's required work is actually satisfied.

External effects

Unknown is not Failed

External work can Succeed, Fail, or be Unknown. If the system cannot tell whether a state-changing effect occurred, it retains that uncertainty and creates reconciliation work rather than pretending failure or retrying blindly.

Tier Three and Tier Four perform the observation. Tier One and Tier Two interpret what the result means.

Negative knowledge

"Not found" is only meaningful inside a complete scope

Ordo distinguishes absent from not searched, not compared, unsupported, unavailable, and unknown. A negative observation can support absence only when the method could detect the target and coverage for the matching scope is Complete.

This prevents missing information from being silently promoted into a fact.

A concrete state example

Explicit state turns business rules into visible routes.

The time-entry experiment is a small example: three named states, four legal transitions, and a terminal state with no route out. The important part is not the domain. It is that the legal graph is inspectable instead of reconstructed from incidental fields and conditionals.

A bounded decision

The decision starts before the model or engineer chooses anything.

Ordo defines the semantic conditions under which a decision is allowed to exist.

{{diagram:decision}}

The important shift is that provider output is never the source of authority. A model may help choose among legal alternatives, but it cannot invent a capability, waive missing coverage, erase an obligation, or make an illegal transition legal.

Architecture

Four tiers. One dependency direction.

Ordo strengthens the semantic core without pulling infrastructure concerns into it.

{{diagram:architecture}}

Dependencies point toward the semantic core. ROS observes engineering execution from outside the application stack. It is not Tier Five, and application correctness must not depend on ROS being present.

AI-assisted engineering

Ordo changes what the agent has to reason about.

The goal is not a smarter prompt. The goal is a smaller, better-defined reasoning surface.

Less reconstruction

Semantic maps, structural locality, explicit state, and bounded context reduce how much of the repository must be rediscovered before a safe change can begin.

A narrower action space

Agents choose from domain-defined legal actions rather than inventing requests and relying on downstream validation to reject the wrong ones.

Explicit refusal and escalation

Missing authority, insufficient coverage, unresolved obligations, and unknown effects can stop work explicitly instead of forcing an answer.

Deterministic work stays deterministic

Where a result can be computed or mechanically verified, Ordo prefers that mechanism over open-ended model reasoning.

{{diagram:context}}

Lower cost, fewer tokens, lower latency, and more safely autonomous work are current measurement objectives, not established Ordo results. The methodology is deliberately instrumented so those claims can be tested rather than assumed.
