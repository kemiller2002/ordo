# Ordo Executable Intelligence Requirements — Pass 2

Status: Second-pass requirements baseline
Purpose: Extend and harden the first-pass executable Ordo requirements by identifying hidden assumptions, edge cases, failure modes, lifecycle concerns, and architectural boundaries that could otherwise be left to implementation guesswork.

This document supplements Pass 1. It does not replace it.

The implementation must satisfy both Pass 1 and Pass 2 unless a later explicitly approved requirement supersedes an earlier one.

---

## 55. Requirement Precedence and Authority

### ORDO-5501 — Requirements must have explicit precedence

When two requirements appear to conflict, the implementation agent shall not invent precedence.

Precedence shall be:

1. Explicitly approved newer Ordo requirements
2. Existing authoritative Ordo methodology documents
3. This Pass 2 document
4. Pass 1 executable Ordo requirements
5. Existing implementation behavior where not contradicted
6. Implementation convenience

Any unresolved conflict must be surfaced as an explicit design issue.

### ORDO-5502 — No implementation-created policy

Implementation code shall not create new policy simply because a case is unspecified.

Unspecified behavior shall remain explicit through typed failure, unsupported state, or unresolved obligation rather than silent inference.

### ORDO-5503 — Defaults must be deliberate

Any default value that affects behavior, security, authority, routing, evidence, confidence, retries, escalation, or transition legality must be documented and tested.

Avoid semantic defaults when omission itself is meaningful.

## 56. Contract Lifecycle

### ORDO-5601 — Decision contracts have lifecycle state

Decision contracts shall be able to distinguish lifecycle states such as:

- Draft
- Active
- Deprecated
- Retired

Exact names may vary, but lifecycle must be explicit where contracts are persisted or reused across versions.

### ORDO-5602 — Deprecated contracts remain interpretable

Historical records created under a deprecated contract must remain understandable.

Deprecation shall not erase historical semantics.

### ORDO-5603 — Retired contracts cannot silently accept new work

A retired contract must not continue receiving new decision requests unless an explicit compatibility rule allows it.

### ORDO-5604 — Contract replacement must be explicit

If Contract A is replaced by Contract B, the relationship should be representable.

Examples:

- supersedes
- compatible replacement
- incompatible replacement
- migration required

### ORDO-5605 — Contract identity is semantic, not physical

Moving a contract between files, assemblies, packages, or namespaces shall not automatically create a new semantic contract identity.

---

## 57. Policy Lifecycle

### ORDO-5701 — Policies may evolve independently of contracts

A decision contract and the policy interpreting that decision are separate concerns.

A contract version change shall not be required merely because a policy threshold changed.

### ORDO-5702 — Policy changes affecting outcomes must be traceable

Historical execution records shall identify the policy version or equivalent policy identity used at decision-acceptance time.

### ORDO-5703 — Policy rollback must be possible

Where policy is externalized or persisted, reverting to a prior approved policy version must be possible without rewriting historical records.

### ORDO-5704 — Policy experiments shall not contaminate production semantics

Experimental policy variants must be explicitly identified and must not silently become production defaults.

---

## 58. State Snapshot Semantics

### ORDO-5801 — Snapshot contents must be intentional

A state snapshot used for decision-making shall contain only state necessary to evaluate the contract.

Do not serialize an entire application object graph merely for convenience.

### ORDO-5802 — Snapshot identity must be stable enough for replay

A snapshot used in a replayable decision must have a stable fingerprint, version, revision, or equivalent identity.

### ORDO-5803 — Snapshot normalization must be deterministic

If snapshots are normalized before hashing or persistence, normalization rules must be deterministic and tested.

### ORDO-5804 — Snapshot hashing must not imply semantic equality

Equal hashes imply equal serialized normalized input, not necessarily equal real-world meaning.

### ORDO-5805 — Sensitive fields may require redaction

A state snapshot may have:

- full internal representation
- provider-safe representation
- persisted audit representation

These need not be identical.

Redaction rules must be explicit.

---

## 59. Evidence Quality

### ORDO-5901 — Evidence may carry quality metadata

Where useful, evidence may identify:

- source authority
- freshness
- confidence
- completeness
- direct vs inferred
- machine-generated vs human-generated

These fields are optional unless required by a domain.

### ORDO-5902 — Evidence quality must not be universally scored by Ordo

Ordo shall not impose a global "evidence score."

Domains define what evidence is trustworthy enough for a decision.

### ORDO-5903 — Derived evidence must identify derivation

If evidence is derived from other evidence, the relationship should be representable.

### ORDO-5904 — Inferred evidence is not direct evidence

A model inference shall not be mislabeled as an observed fact.

### ORDO-5905 — Conflicting evidence must be representable

The evidence model must allow mutually inconsistent evidence to coexist until resolved.

### ORDO-5906 — Evidence supersession must not erase history

New evidence may supersede prior evidence for current decisions, but historical decision context must remain reconstructable.

---

## 60. Decision Input Discipline

### ORDO-6001 — Inputs shall be deterministic when possible

The process that constructs a DecisionRequest from domain state shall be deterministic unless explicitly documented otherwise.

### ORDO-6002 — Prompt rendering is not the contract

If an AI provider requires textual prompting, the generated prompt is an adapter representation of the contract, not the contract itself.

### ORDO-6003 — Provider prompts must be reproducible where practical

Given the same:

- contract version
- state snapshot
- evidence
- provider adapter version

the adapter should be able to recreate the same logical request representation.

### ORDO-6004 — Hidden provider instructions must be controlled

Provider adapters shall not contain undocumented behavioral instructions that materially alter domain semantics.

### ORDO-6005 — Prompt changes affecting semantics require version awareness

If provider prompt construction materially changes the interpretation of a decision contract, the change must be observable through adapter or request versioning.

---

## 61. Decision Output Discipline

### ORDO-6101 — Parsing failures are contract failures

If a provider returns an answer that cannot be safely parsed into the required typed result, the operation fails explicitly.

Do not repair arbitrary provider output through guesswork.

### ORDO-6102 — Repair behavior must be explicit

If structured-output repair is supported, it must be:

- bounded
- deterministic where possible
- observable
- non-semantic

Repair may fix syntax.

Repair may not choose a domain answer on the provider's behalf.

### ORDO-6103 — Provider explanations are secondary artifacts

A provider may optionally return explanation or rationale.

The authoritative result remains the typed decision outcome.

### ORDO-6104 — Explanations must not override typed results

If prose explanation conflicts with the typed result, the conflict must be surfaced.

Do not reinterpret the typed result from prose.

---

## 62. Provider Disagreement

### ORDO-6201 — Multiple provider results may coexist

Ordo shall permit multiple decisions to be recorded against the same request.

### ORDO-6202 — Provider disagreement must be explicit

If providers disagree, Ordo shall not silently merge their answers.

### ORDO-6203 — Consensus is domain policy

Voting, majority rule, weighted consensus, unanimity, adjudication, and tie-breaking are domain or orchestration policies.

Ordo shall not define a universal consensus algorithm.

### ORDO-6204 — Independent verification must identify independence

If a second provider is used for verification, the system should record whether it is:

- same provider, same model
- same provider, different model
- different provider
- deterministic verifier
- human verifier

### ORDO-6205 — Correlated providers are not assumed independent

Using two frontier models does not prove independent evidence.

---

## 63. Confidence Semantics

### ORDO-6301 — Confidence scale must be defined

If confidence uses a numeric scale, the valid range and interpretation must be explicit.

### ORDO-6302 — Confidence absence is valid

Providers incapable of meaningful confidence must be allowed to return no confidence.

### ORDO-6303 — Confidence must not be fabricated by adapters

An adapter shall not synthesize a confidence value merely to satisfy a downstream schema.

### ORDO-6304 — Transformed confidence must preserve provenance

If a provider score is mapped or normalized, the transformed value must identify that transformation.

### ORDO-6305 — Confidence thresholds require enough evidence before optimization

Thresholds should not be tuned from tiny samples without recording the evidence base used to justify them.

### ORDO-6306 — Confidence is scoped to a contract/domain

A model calibrated for one decision contract must not be assumed calibrated for another.

---

## 64. Outcome Semantics

### ORDO-6401 — Outcome type must be domain-defined

Ordo shall not assume every decision has a binary correct/incorrect outcome.

### ORDO-6402 — Outcome may remain unresolved forever

Some decisions may never receive objective ground truth.

This must be representable.

### ORDO-6403 — Outcome source must be explicit

An outcome should identify whether it came from:

- human judgment
- automated verification
- observed production state
- subsequent event
- external authoritative source
- consensus process

### ORDO-6404 — Outcome disagreement must be representable

Two outcome sources may disagree.

Do not overwrite one with another.

### ORDO-6405 — Outcome correction must preserve history

If a previously recorded outcome is later corrected, the original must remain auditable.

---

## 65. Human Review

### ORDO-6501 — Human review is a typed state

"Waiting for human review" shall be represented explicitly.

### ORDO-6502 — Human reviewers require capability

Not every human user is automatically authorized to override every decision.

### ORDO-6503 — Human override requires reason capture where consequential

Domains may require an override reason.

The mechanism must support this.

### ORDO-6504 — Human confidence is optional

Human reviewers shall not be forced to invent a numerical confidence value.

### ORDO-6505 — Human review must distinguish decision from authorization

A human may agree with a decision yet lack authority to execute the resulting transition.

### ORDO-6506 — Human review latency is observable

Systems such as ROS may measure time waiting for human review.

Ordo shall expose the necessary lifecycle events.

---

## 66. Cancellation and Timeouts

### ORDO-6601 — Long-running operations must support cancellation

Provider and deliberation operations should support cancellation through standard .NET cancellation mechanisms.

### ORDO-6602 — Cancellation is distinct from failure

Cancelled work shall not be recorded as provider failure unless the provider itself failed.

### ORDO-6603 — Timeouts must be explicit

Provider adapters may define timeouts.

Timeouts must be configurable or policy-driven where appropriate.

### ORDO-6604 — Timeout behavior must not silently escalate

A timeout may trigger an escalation policy, but the escalation must be recorded explicitly.

### ORDO-6605 — Partial results must not be assumed valid

If cancellation or timeout occurs after partial provider output, that output is invalid unless the provider contract explicitly supports partial results.

---

## 67. Retry Semantics

### ORDO-6701 — Transport retries and semantic retries differ

Transport retry:

- retries equivalent request due to transient infrastructure failure.

Semantic retry:

- asks for a new decision because the first answer was unacceptable.

These must not be conflated.

### ORDO-6702 — Semantic retry requires explicit policy

Repeatedly asking until the desired answer appears is prohibited.

### ORDO-6703 — Retry counts must be observable

Adapters should expose retry activity for ROS/telemetry.

### ORDO-6704 — Retry must preserve causality

Each retry shall remain linked to the original request.

---

## 68. Side Effects

### ORDO-6801 — Decision evaluation is side-effect free with respect to domain state

Evaluating a decision must not directly mutate authoritative domain state.

### ORDO-6802 — Deliberation capabilities may cause side effects only when explicitly granted

For example, a deliberative agent may be allowed to:

- read repository files
- run tests
- query systems

but not necessarily:

- deploy
- delete
- write production data

Capabilities must control these boundaries.

### ORDO-6803 — Side-effect requests must be distinguishable from side-effect completion

A request to perform an effect is not proof the effect occurred.

### ORDO-6804 — External effect failure must not corrupt domain transition semantics

Applications must be able to represent:

- transition succeeded, effect pending
- effect failed
- compensation required
- transition rolled back where supported

Ordo shall provide compatible primitives without becoming a saga/workflow framework.

---

## 69. Partial Failure

### ORDO-6901 — Multi-step resolution may partially succeed

The model shall support cases where:

- evidence was acquired
- one decision completed
- another failed
- transition was not executed

### ORDO-6902 — Partial progress must remain observable

Do not collapse partially completed work into a single generic failure.

### ORDO-6903 — Recovery obligations may be generated

A partial failure may create obligations such as:

- retry provider
- reacquire stale evidence
- human review
- compensate external effect
- abandon operation

---

## 70. Idempotency and Duplicate Processing

### ORDO-7001 — Decision request identity must support duplicate detection where needed

Ordo shall permit applications or ROS to recognize repeated processing of the same logical request.

### ORDO-7002 — Duplicate decision execution is not automatically an error

Replay, verification, and comparison intentionally duplicate evaluation.

Purpose must be explicit.

### ORDO-7003 — Duplicate transitions must be prevented or handled by domain policy

Ordo shall not assume transitions are idempotent.

---

## 71. Causality

### ORDO-7101 — Every derived operation should be linkable to its cause

Examples:

- decision caused by task
- deliberation caused by low confidence
- human review caused by policy
- transition caused by accepted decision

### ORDO-7102 — Correlation and causation IDs should remain distinct

A broad execution correlation ID may contain multiple causal branches.

### ORDO-7103 — Causal chains must survive persistence boundaries

If ROS stores the execution history, enough identity must survive to reconstruct causal structure.

---

## 72. Event and Observation Model

### ORDO-7201 — Observation events describe facts

Observation events shall state what happened.

They shall not embed analytics conclusions such as:

- model is best
- decision was wasteful
- should retrain

Those are downstream interpretations.

### ORDO-7202 — Observation event schemas require versioning

Persisted observation events need stable schemas.

### ORDO-7203 — Observation must not change behavior

Instrumentation failures shall not normally change domain behavior unless an application explicitly requires audit persistence before proceeding.

### ORDO-7204 — Required audit and optional telemetry must be distinguishable

Some domains may require audit records for legal or operational reasons.

Optional performance telemetry is not equivalent.

---

## 73. Auditability

### ORDO-7301 — Critical decisions must be explainable through recorded facts

Audit reconstruction should be able to answer:

- what state was evaluated
- what contract was used
- what evidence was available
- who/what made the decision
- what result was returned
- what policy evaluated it
- what transition followed
- what verification occurred

### ORDO-7302 — Audit history must be append-oriented

Historical decision records should not be casually overwritten.

### ORDO-7303 — Corrections must be additive

Corrections should append a correction/supersession relationship rather than mutate historical truth invisibly.

### ORDO-7304 — Auditability must not require retaining hidden chain-of-thought

The system shall not depend on private model reasoning traces.

Typed outputs, evidence references, provider metadata, and optional concise rationale are sufficient.

---

## 74. Privacy and Data Minimization

### ORDO-7401 — Provider-bound data must be minimized

The decision request sent to a provider should contain no more sensitive data than required.

### ORDO-7402 — Audit retention and provider transmission may differ

Information needed internally for audit may still be prohibited from external-provider transmission.

### ORDO-7403 — Redaction must be deterministic where possible

If data is redacted before provider use, redaction behavior should be testable.

### ORDO-7404 — Replays must respect current access controls

The ability to replay a historical decision does not automatically grant access to all original sensitive evidence.

---

## 75. Security Boundary

### ORDO-7501 — Provider output is untrusted input

All provider responses shall be validated against the contract.

### ORDO-7502 — Provider prose shall never be executed as code or commands by default

Any executable action proposed by a provider must pass through typed capability and policy boundaries.

### ORDO-7503 — Tool invocation from deliberation must be capability-scoped

If a deliberative provider can call tools, the set of allowed tools/actions must be explicit.

### ORDO-7504 — Provider-generated identifiers are not trusted identities

Provider output cannot establish authoritative user IDs, resource IDs, capabilities, or credentials without verification.

---

## 76. Prompt Injection and Untrusted Evidence

### ORDO-7601 — Evidence may contain adversarial instructions

Text contained in evidence must be treated as data unless the contract explicitly says otherwise.

### ORDO-7602 — External content must not override system authority

Repository files, emails, documents, web pages, issue text, and similar sources cannot alter Ordo policy merely by containing instructions.

### ORDO-7603 — Provider adapters should separate instructions from evidence

Where provider APIs permit it, system instructions, contract instructions, and evidence content should be structurally separated.

### ORDO-7604 — Injection-sensitive operations require tests

At least one provider conformance test should include malicious or misleading evidence content.

---

## 77. Deliberation Scope Control

### ORDO-7701 — Deliberation has a bounded objective

A deliberation request must specify what problem it is authorized to investigate.

### ORDO-7702 — Deliberation context expansion must be observable

If an agent expands from local context to broader repository/system context, that expansion should be recorded where practical.

### ORDO-7703 — Deliberation must terminate with a typed status

Examples:

- Resolved
- RequiresDecision
- RequiresEvidence
- RequiresHuman
- Blocked
- Failed
- Cancelled

### ORDO-7704 — Deliberation shall not silently create new objectives

Newly discovered work should become an explicit obligation or proposed work item.

---

## 78. Deliberation Budgeting

### ORDO-7801 — Deliberation may have resource budgets

Requests may define limits such as:

- time
- provider cost
- tool calls
- context expansion
- retries

### ORDO-7802 — Budget exhaustion is distinct from reasoning failure

A task that stops because it reached its budget must be represented separately from an unresolved logical failure.

### ORDO-7803 — Budget extension requires explicit authority

A provider may request more budget but cannot silently expand it.

---

## 79. Deterministic Computation Registry

### ORDO-7901 — Computations should be named where reused

Reusable deterministic computations should have stable identities where they participate in decision evidence or replay.

### ORDO-7902 — Computation version may matter

If a computation algorithm materially changes, historical decisions may need to identify which version produced evidence.

### ORDO-7903 — Computation output can be evidence

Mechanically derived values may be attached as evidence with derivation provenance.

### ORDO-7904 — Do not create a generic rule engine

Named deterministic computation support shall not evolve into a universal DSL/rules engine without explicit justification.

---

## 80. Deliberate → Decide Promotion

### ORDO-8001 — Promotion requires evidence

A repeated deliberative pattern shall not automatically become a DecisionContract.

Before promotion, document:

- repeated task shape
- bounded output space
- required evidence
- observed outcomes
- failure modes
- residual ambiguity

### ORDO-8002 — Promotion creates a new explicit contract

A promoted bounded decision must have its own contract identity/version.

### ORDO-8003 — Deliberation fallback may remain

Promoting a problem to Decide does not require eliminating Deliberate as an escalation path.

---

## 81. Decide → Compute Promotion

### ORDO-8101 — Deterministic promotion requires stronger evidence

A bounded decision should only become Compute if the result can be mechanically derived from stable inputs.

High observed model accuracy alone is insufficient.

### ORDO-8102 — Deterministic rule must be explainable

The deterministic condition must be representable in code/tests independently from historical model outputs.

### ORDO-8103 — Promotion should reduce provider dependency

Once safely deterministic, the normal path should no longer require AI for that computation.

---

## 82. Experimentation Boundaries

### ORDO-8201 — Experimental behavior must be labeled

Experimental contracts, providers, policies, and routing behavior shall be identifiable.

### ORDO-8202 — Experiment execution must not alter production policy silently

A/B or replay experiments should default to non-mutating execution unless explicitly authorized.

### ORDO-8203 — Experimental outputs remain separate from authoritative decisions

Shadow-provider results must not silently become production decisions.

### ORDO-8204 — Experiment metadata may be attached externally

Ordo shall support experiment identifiers without depending on the research/ROS implementation.

---

## 83. Reproducibility

### ORDO-8301 — Perfect provider replay must not be promised

External model providers may change behavior even with identical input.

### ORDO-8302 — Logical replay and exact replay are distinct

Logical replay means:

- same contract
- same recorded state/evidence
- new provider invocation

Exact replay may be impossible.

### ORDO-8303 — Deterministic fixtures support exact regression tests

Tests requiring stable outputs shall use fake/recorded providers rather than live AI.

---

## 84. Serialization and Schema Evolution

### ORDO-8401 — Union serialization must be stable

Discriminated unions crossing persistence boundaries require explicit stable representations.

### ORDO-8402 — F# type names are not sufficient wire contracts

Renaming a CLR/F# type shall not necessarily break persisted data.

### ORDO-8403 — Schema migrations must be tested

Persisted records from prior supported versions must have compatibility tests.

### ORDO-8404 — Unknown future variants require explicit handling

Older consumers must either:

- safely reject unknown variants
- preserve them opaquely
- use a documented compatibility strategy

They must not silently reinterpret them.

---

## 85. Cross-Language Consumption

### ORDO-8501 — F# is the reference implementation

The authoritative implementation is F#.

### ORDO-8502 — Wire contracts should remain language-neutral

Persisted/public schemas should not require F# runtime semantics to interpret.

### ORDO-8503 — Cross-language clients must not weaken semantics

If C#, TypeScript, Python, or other consumers are added, they must preserve:

- contract identity
- legal choices
- uncertainty states
- evidence semantics
- authority boundaries

### ORDO-8504 — Cross-language SDKs are deferred

Do not build SDKs until a real consumer requires them.

---

## 86. Package and Dependency Boundaries

### ORDO-8601 — Core dependency graph must remain acyclic

Project/package dependencies shall be simple and documented.

### ORDO-8602 — Provider adapters depend inward

Provider packages may depend on Ordo abstractions.

Core abstractions shall never depend outward on provider packages.

### ORDO-8603 — Testing helpers must not become runtime dependencies

Production packages shall not depend on test packages.

### ORDO-8604 — ROS integration must remain optional

An application can use executable Ordo without installing ROS.

---

## 87. Public API Stability

### ORDO-8701 — Public API is smaller than implementation API

Only stable, reusable concepts should be public.

### ORDO-8702 — Internal experimentation remains internal

Do not publish types simply because tests or experiments use them.

### ORDO-8703 — Breaking API changes require explicit versioning/migration

NuGet or equivalent package versioning shall follow existing Echelon Foundry release conventions.

---

## 88. Performance Isolation

### ORDO-8801 — Core primitives must not allocate excessively

Hot-path state/decision primitives should be reviewed for unnecessary allocations where relevant.

### ORDO-8802 — Performance optimization must follow measurement

Do not compromise semantic clarity for hypothetical micro-optimization.

### ORDO-8803 — Provider latency must not block unrelated computations unnecessarily

The architecture should permit asynchronous provider operations.

### ORDO-8804 — Parallel decisions require explicit independence

Multiple decisions may execute concurrently only when domain dependencies permit it.

---

## 89. Parallelism

### ORDO-8901 — Parallel evaluation must not imply parallel transitions

Decisions may be evaluated concurrently while transitions remain ordered by domain rules.

### ORDO-8902 — Shared mutable state must be avoided

Parallel provider evaluation should operate against immutable snapshots.

### ORDO-8903 — Conflicting parallel results require explicit reconciliation

Do not choose "last completed" as an implicit winner.

---

## 90. Caching

### ORDO-9001 — Decision caching is not universally safe

Ordo shall not cache provider decisions by default.

### ORDO-9002 — Cache eligibility is contract/domain policy

Caching may be allowed only when:

- relevant state/evidence is identical
- contract semantics are identical
- provider/model differences are acceptable
- freshness constraints are satisfied

### ORDO-9003 — Cached result provenance must remain visible

Consumers must know whether a decision was newly evaluated or reused.

### ORDO-9004 — Cache must not bypass state-version checks

---

## 91. Fallback Providers

### ORDO-9101 — Fallback must be explicit

Provider fallback chains must be configured, not hidden inside adapters.

### ORDO-9102 — Fallback may change semantics

Switching providers may change:

- confidence availability
- calibration
- latency
- cost
- output behavior

These differences must remain observable.

### ORDO-9103 — Security boundary must be checked before fallback

A fallback provider may have a different data-processing trust boundary.

Evidence must not be sent automatically if policy prohibits it.

---

## 92. Provider Capability Negotiation

### ORDO-9201 — Adapters expose supported features

Provider adapters should declare capabilities explicitly.

### ORDO-9202 — Unsupported capability fails early

If a contract requires a capability a provider lacks, fail before sending the request where possible.

### ORDO-9203 — Feature degradation must be explicit

Example:

A provider lacking calibrated probability support may still supply a decision if the policy permits confidence to be absent.

This must not be silently transformed into equivalent functionality.

---

## 93. Domain Consequence Modeling

### ORDO-9301 — Consequence can influence policy

Applications may classify operations by consequence/risk.

### ORDO-9302 — Ordo shall not define universal risk levels

If Ordo provides a generic consequence abstraction, domain-specific interpretation remains external.

### ORDO-9303 — High consequence may require stronger verification

Applications may require:

- independent verification
- human approval
- multiple capabilities
- fresh evidence

regardless of provider confidence.

---

## 94. Obligation Lifecycle

### ORDO-9401 — Obligations require stable identity

Persistent obligations shall have IDs.

### ORDO-9402 — Obligation states must be explicit

Examples:

- Open
- Satisfied
- Cancelled
- Superseded
- Blocked

### ORDO-9403 — Satisfied obligations preserve completion evidence

Completion should record how the obligation was satisfied.

### ORDO-9404 — Obligations may depend on other obligations

Dependency relationships may be represented, but Ordo shall not become a generic project-management engine.

---

## 95. Staleness

### ORDO-9501 — Evidence may become stale

Contracts/policies may define freshness requirements.

### ORDO-9502 — Stale evidence is distinct from missing evidence

### ORDO-9503 — Provider decision may become stale independently

Even if evidence remains valid, domain state may change and invalidate the decision.

### ORDO-9504 — Freshness should be domain-defined

Ordo shall not impose universal time-based expiration rules.

---

## 96. Clock and Time Semantics

### ORDO-9601 — Time-sensitive rules need an injectable clock

Core behavior that depends on time should use an explicit clock abstraction or function.

### ORDO-9602 — Tests must not depend on wall-clock time

### ORDO-9603 — Persist timestamps in unambiguous form

Use UTC/offset-aware representations according to existing .NET conventions.

### ORDO-9604 — Timestamp is not ordering proof in distributed systems

Use revisions/causal identifiers where ordering correctness matters.

## 97. Deterministic IDs

### ORDO-9701 — ID generation strategy must be deliberate

Some identifiers may be random.

Others may benefit from deterministic derivation.

The reason shall be explicit.

### ORDO-9702 — IDs shall not encode sensitive data

### ORDO-9703 — Human-readable labels are not primary identity

Contract names may change without losing identity.

### ORDO-9801 — Core shall not require a logging framework

### ORDO-9802 — Structured observation is preferred over textual logging for semantic events

### ORDO-9803 — Logs must not be required to reconstruct authoritative domain state

### ORDO-9804 — Sensitive decision/evidence content must not be blindly logged

### ORDO-9902 — Diagnostic detail should support agents and humans

## 100. Testing Depth

### ORDO-10001 — Property-style tests should be considered for invariants

Examples:

- provider cannot produce illegal choice accepted by core
- stale decision cannot authorize changed state
- missing capability always blocks protected transition
- replay never mutates domain state

### ORDO-10002 — Mutation testing may be used where already supported

Do not introduce a heavy mutation framework solely for this feature unless justified.

### ORDO-10003 — Concurrency tests are required for stale-state protection

### ORDO-10004 — Cancellation and timeout tests are required

### ORDO-10005 — Contract-version compatibility tests are required

### ORDO-10006 — Prompt-injection/adversarial evidence tests are required for AI adapters

### ORDO-10007 — Provider response corruption tests are required

Test cases should include:

- malformed JSON
- invalid enum
- extra fields
- missing required fields
- contradictory explanation
- no confidence
- invalid confidence range

Documentation must explain what the primitive means, not merely repeat the type name.

### ORDO-10202 — Document authority chain

The docs must make clear:

Provider result
→ policy
→ capability/evidence checks
→ transition

Provider result
→ action

### ORDO-10204 — Document replay limitations

### ORDO-10206 — Document migration examples

Deliberate → Decide
Decide → Compute

- LLM used for arithmetic

## 106. Agent Implementation Guardrails

### ORDO-10601 — Agent must inspect before modifying

Before implementation, the agent must inspect:

- repository structure
- existing Ordo contracts
- current tests
- package conventions
- current serialization approach
- existing provider abstractions, if any
- ROS integration points, if any

### ORDO-10602 — Agent must create a baseline

Record:

- commit SHA
- build status
- test status
- architecture checks
- relevant metrics

before changes.

### ORDO-10603 — Agent must not invent missing requirements silently

Ambiguities that can materially change architecture must be recorded as open design questions or resolved from existing authoritative documents.

### ORDO-10604 — Agent must implement vertical slices

Prefer:

contract
→ provider
→ outcome
→ policy
→ transition
→ observation
→ tests

over broad horizontal scaffolding with no end-to-end behavior.

### ORDO-10605 — Agent must minimize unrelated changes

### ORDO-10606 — Agent must preserve working behavior

### ORDO-10607 — Agent must provide evidence of completion

Completion report shall include:

- files changed
- contracts added
- tests added
- build result
- test result
- architecture verification
- known limitations
- deferred items
- any requirement not fully satisfied

## 109. Architectural Invariants

The implementation shall preserve all of the following invariants.

### Invariant A

A provider may suggest information.

A provider does not own domain state.

### Invariant B

A decision is not a transition.

### Invariant C

Confidence is not authority.

### Invariant D

Evidence is not automatically truth.

### Invariant E

A successful provider call is not a successful domain operation.

### Invariant F

Deliberation is not permission.

### Invariant G

Human review is not automatically authorization.

### Invariant H

Replay is not execution.

### Invariant I

Historical correction does not erase history.

### Invariant J

Missing evidence is not low confidence.

### Invariant K

Provider disagreement is not consensus.

### Invariant L

Repeated AI agreement is not proof that a rule is deterministic.

### Invariant M

A newer contract is not automatically semantically compatible with an older contract.

### Invariant N

Ordo defines reusable semantics.

Applications define domain truth.

ROS observes and optimizes execution.

## 110. Second-Pass Design Principle

The second-pass implementation shall optimize for containment of uncertainty and failure.

The desired architecture is:

Domain state
    ↓
Explicit contract
    ↓
Controlled resolution boundary
    ↓
Validated structured result
    ↓
Evidence + policy + capability + state-version checks
    ↓
Authorized transition
    ↓
Observed outcome

Failures must remain typed and local.

Uncertainty must remain visible.

External intelligence must remain replaceable.

Historical behavior must remain reconstructable.

No provider, agent, retry mechanism, fallback path, or convenience abstraction may bypass domain authority.

---

## 111. Final Pass-2 Constraint

Any implementation choice that makes the system easier to use by hiding one of the following must be presumed incorrect until explicitly justified:

- uncertainty
- missing evidence
- authority
- state version
- provider identity
- contract version
- policy version
- failure
- escalation
- side effects
- historical causality

Convenience is not sufficient reason to hide semantic state.
